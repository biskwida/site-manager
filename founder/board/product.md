# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one sentence already exists:** "Text your café's website like you text a waiter." The core moment works well. The owner sends "cappuccino is 3.20 now" in a chat, sees a preview, taps once, and the change is live. It starts from the owner's day, not from a CMS.
- **The trust loop is designed in.** Preview, one tap, change log, undo and auto-revert are the details that let a non-technical owner relax. They are the craft that most competitors leave out.
- **It is real.** It runs in production, one venue (Coffique) serves its live menu from it, and there are 435 tests. The parts nobody sees are getting real attention.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** For one solo founder, the scope is three templates, a 7-step wizard, its own Vercel deploy, a WordPress plugin, three chat channels (a fourth is pending), three tiers, change quotas, multi-location support, and planned Google and Instagram add-ons. Each one is a seam to maintain. The one thing that must be brilliant is changing the menu by chat. Everything else is diluting it before ten venues pay.
2. **The website may be the wrong surface.** Diners often check Google, Instagram or Facebook, not the venue's site. If a chat-driven price change does not reach those places, the owner still has stale information where customers actually look. The planned Google and Instagram add-ons hint that the real one thing may be "my information is correct everywhere." That is a different product from "a website."
3. **Seams at onboarding and in the chat.** The first five minutes go: pick a template, fill a wizard, deploy, then connect a bot in a separate step. That is a chain of handoffs, and each step is a chance to drop off. Viber, the messenger Bulgarian owners use most, is missing, so many owners have to adopt a new app just to use the product. One misread price that goes live (from a photo, a voice note or Bulgarian text) costs the owner's trust in a way that is hard to win back.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the single thing, and what would you cut tomorrow to protect it?
- How long does a new owner take, from first contact to first live change? Who has done it without your help?
- How many changes per week does Coffique actually send? Does the owner come back unprompted?
- Which chat app do your four quoted venues use every day? How many of them would switch to get this?
- What happens when the agent misreads a price, and how often has that happened?
- Does a site change matter if Google and Instagram still show the old price?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Cut to one template, one onboarding path that you lead yourself, and the chat apps the first buyers already use. Freeze the WordPress plugin and the tiers until there are 10 paying venues.
- Show 3 to 5 venues that went from first contact to first live change in under 15 minutes, and that keep using the product weekly for a month.
- Measure how accurate the agent's changes are on real messages. Publish the error rate, and how undo works, to owners.
- Decide whether the product is "a website" or "correct everywhere," and design the experience around that answer.
