# Monopoly lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The small market to start in is clear:** owner-run cafés, bars and small restaurants in Sofia, with Bulgarian-language chat. A solo founder could plausibly win a large share of a niche this narrow before global website builders bother to localise for it.
- **The interface is a real difference, not just a cheaper price:** a voice note or a photo of the specials board becomes a previewed live change with one tap, plus scheduling, auto-revert and undo. For an owner with no web person, that may be close to ten times easier than logging into a site builder. It still has to be proven in daily use.
- **Distribution has started:** the founder already knows the venues, one real venue (Coffique) runs on it, and four price conversations ranged from 20 to 50+ €.
- **The economics point the right way:** 25 € list price against roughly 6.4 € of AI cost per venue, and fixed costs that get cheaper per venue as the count grows.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable moat.** The technology is general-purpose AI on top of standard hosting. Any website builder, POS vendor, delivery platform or local agency could add "edit by chat" within months. There are no network effects today, and the brand is one demo café. What stops a copycat in month six is still unanswered.
2. **Distribution that does not scale past the founder's contacts.** Door-to-door selling to friendly venues gets the first ten customers, not the first hundred. CAC and the referral rate are unknown, and a 15–25 € plan cannot carry much field-sales time.
3. **A ceiling on the market, and a platform it doesn't own.** Owners already live on Instagram, Facebook and Google profiles. If they keep treating the website as optional, the product competes for a shrinking slot. Viber, the main messenger in Bulgaria, is priced out, and Meta channels depend on Meta's rules.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about how these owners work that a global builder would not find out?
- Which advantage can this build: structured menu data across many venues (a diner-facing directory or a supplier-pricing network effect), owning the Google, Instagram and site updates in one place, or a local brand?
- How many independent venues are in Sofia and Bulgaria, and what share is realistic in 24 months?
- Who sells websites or digital menus to these venues today, and at what price?
- What does each of the next 50 customers cost to win, and how many come through referrals?
- Why now: is it simply that voice and vision AI only recently became cheap and accurate enough in Bulgarian?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Turn the four verbal quotes into at least 10 paying venues, and show that they actually use it weekly and keep paying.
2. Name a moat with a credible path to it, for example becoming the one place a venue's details are updated across site, Google and Instagram, or a data or network play built on menus across venues.
3. Measure CAC and the referral rate on the next 20 sign-ups, and show a channel that does not need the founder in the room.
4. Size the beachhead: count the venues and map the competitors' prices.
