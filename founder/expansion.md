## The short version

- **Ten more countries, about 191,000 food-service businesses**, roughly 8× Bulgaria (22,756). Greece alone is 76,728.
- **Nobody in the region updates a venue's site by chat.** Every local competitor is a QR menu edited in an admin panel, priced €3–65 a month. The only regional player, doXmenu (Skopje), sells one plan at €12.99–14.99 in all six Western Balkan countries.
- **Sell through local partners**, mostly till/POS resellers, who invoice locally in the local currency. In Romania, Serbia, Kosovo and Bosnia this is required, not just convenient: an unregistered café can't easily buy a service from an EU company directly.
- **Order:** Bulgaria → Romania + Serbia → Greece + Montenegro → Croatia + Albania + Kosovo → Bosnia + North Macedonia + Slovenia.
- **Base case (1% of each market two years after entry):** about **3,050 venues in year 5**, 69,500 € a month in revenue, about **543,000 € a year profit before salaries**. Cautious: 1,700 venues, 326,000 €. Strong: 5,760 venues, 978,000 €.

## Why this order

1. **Romania:** the second-biggest market, low seasonality (peak month 1.36× the quietest), WhatsApp (already built), local QR menus cost €7–16, and the closest full-site rental costs €140 a month. The catch: RON prices and the D700 VAT code for unregistered buyers, so a local partner invoices.
2. **Serbia:** a big market with high wages for the region (€1,034 net), and a clear partner channel through fiscal POS vendors (Petcom, IntelliPOS, HARDoffice, mts) and the HORES association. It needs **Viber**, which then serves every later Viber country.
3. **Greece:** the biggest prize (3× Bulgaria) and the biggest website gap (43.8%). Year 3, not year 2, because of the risks: strong seasonality (turnover doubles from February to August; islands hold 24% of sites), Greek price-list rules (Greek, VAT included, fines from €500 per item), and Viber/Messenger usage. **Timing:** small Greek firms must start e-invoicing from 2 November 2026, so accountants and till resellers are calling every venue right now. That's the partner opening.
4. **Montenegro:** same language and Viber bot as Serbia, and the euro, so it's cheap to add. Coastal and seasonal.
5. **Croatia, Albania, Kosovo:** Croatia is coastal (57% of venues) and seasonal, with fiscal till resellers as the channel (Monri/Remaris, Tehno Sistem). Albania and Kosovo are one Albanian-language launch.
6. **Bosnia, North Macedonia, Slovenia:** smaller, harder (Bosnia has two tax systems, North Macedonia is doXmenu's home turf, Slovenia already has 87.5% websites and no named partners). Enter only when a partner shows up.

## Who sells it: local partners

Outside Bulgaria we don't sell door to door. **Partners who already visit cafés and restaurants for their own business** sell Site Manager alongside what they offer today.

| Partner | Why they fit |
| --- | --- |
| **Till / POS resellers (the strongest)** | Every venue must have a fiscal till, so these people know every owner and visit regularly. In Greece they're calling venues right now for the new e-invoicing rules (small firms from 2 November 2026). |
| Accountants for restaurants | They talk to the owner every month, and owners trust them. |
| Small web agencies | They already sell websites; Site Manager gives them a cheaper site that the owner keeps up alone. |
| Food and drink suppliers | Their sales reps are in the venue every week. |
| Hospitality associations | They don't sell, but they can recommend it to members. |

**What they get:** 15% of the monthly fee for as long as the venue stays (a partner with 50 venues at 25 € earns about 190 € a month) and half the setup fee for doing the setup visit. They also get something new to sell to customers they already have.

**What they do:** show it to their venues and set them up; **invoice the venue locally**, in local currency (in Romania, Serbia, Kosovo and Bosnia this solves a real VAT problem for small unregistered cafés); and be the local person the owner can call, which answers the buyer panel's "who do I call if it breaks?". We run the product and the AI, invoice the partner monthly, and they pay us by SEPA transfer.

**Why it matters:** growth without the founder in the room (5 venues a month from one partner is the base case, 1 a month the cautious case); trust, because owners buy from people they know; and it costs about 4 € per venue per month instead of a sales salary.

**How to start:** one partner per country first, 10 free setups to sell, and count the venues a month they bring. Test it in Sofia first with a till reseller or restaurant accountant: the cheapest way to learn whether partners sell before going abroad.

## Five years, all countries including Bulgaria

Bulgaria follows the plan's ramp (31 → 100 → 250 → 300 → 350 venues, **estimate**). Each new country reaches half its target in its first year and the full target in its second, then grows by half a target a year. Costs: 112 € a month fixed, Viber 100 € a month from year 2, about 1,000 € per country to launch (translation, travel, partner training; **estimate**). **No salaries.**

**What these numbers leave out:**
- **People.** From year 2 you need help: someone for support in each language group, and someone managing partners. Count roughly one support person per 500–700 venues plus a partner manager from year 3 (**estimate**). In the base case that's 3–6 people by year 5, which takes a large part of the profit above.
- **Closures.** 6–12% of food businesses close every year (Eurostat: Greece 5.9%, Romania 9.5%, Croatia 7.2%, Bulgaria 12.1%), so the counts are net and growth has to replace them. Seasonal venues may pause in winter.
- **Tax.** 10% Bulgarian corporate tax, 5% on dividends.

## The first step for each country

Find **one partner** before building anything country-specific: a till/POS reseller or restaurant accountant who already visits venues. Give them 10 free setups to sell, and measure how many venues a month they bring. A partner who brings 5 a month makes the base case; 1 a month makes the cautious case.

Named candidates from the research:
- **Serbia:** Petcom, IntelliPOS, HARDoffice, mts, the HORES association.
- **Greece:** Kentro Tameiakis, RBS/NINE POS, the Epsilon Net reseller network, the POESE restaurant federation.
- **Croatia:** Monri/Remaris and its distributor Tehno Sistem.
- **Albania:** easyPos, Logic, AED.

The research has no named partners for North Macedonia, Bosnia or Montenegro.

The panel and the numbers are projections. Not financial, legal or tax advice: the VAT, e-invoicing and fiscalisation points need a local accountant in each country.
