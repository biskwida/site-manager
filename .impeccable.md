## Design Context

### Users
Two audiences. (1) Café, bar and small-restaurant owners in Bulgaria, mostly non-technical, on a phone, between customers; they use the wizard once (10–20 minutes, often in the evening) and then the Telegram agent daily. They need to trust that the thing they are building will look like a real business site, and that nothing goes live without their tap. (2) Their guests: locals and tourists on phones, often standing outside the venue or on a map, asking "is it open, what do they have, how much, where is it". Bilingual Bulgarian/English with Bulgarian first.

### Brand Personality
Calm, capable, local. Site Manager is a trusted helper from the neighbourhood, not a SaaS dashboard: it speaks plainly, never hypes, shows what it will do before it does it, and gets out of the way. Emotional goals: confidence ("this will look right"), relief ("I don't have to touch the site again"), and quiet pride when the owner shows the site to a friend.

### Aesthetic Direction
References: the craft of Linear and Vercel (precision, restraint, detail-obsession, fast feedback), applied to a warm local context rather than a developer tool. Anti-references: Wix/Squarespace template look, Bootstrap admin panels, the purple-gradient "AI product" look, neon on black, glassmorphism, icon-above-heading card grids.
Site Manager brand (wizard, dashboard, landing): light theme (used in daylight, in a café, on a phone); one brand hue chosen in OKLCH with all neutrals tinted toward it (no pure black or white); a distinctive display face paired with a quiet body face, both with Cyrillic subsets; no pre-existing logo, a wordmark is fine. The current `#2f6b4f` green is a venue accent default, not the brand hue.
Venue templates are deliberately different from each other and from the brand: `classic` warm editorial paper, `nocturne` dark cinematic for bars and evenings, `lumen` light photographic for daytime coffee. Theme follows the venue's hours and mood, not a default.

### Design Principles
1. Show before you change: every state (draft, preview, scheduled, live) is visible and named; no silent writes.
2. Bulgarian first, both natural: copy is written in each language, never translated word for word; Cyrillic is a typographic requirement, not an afterthought.
3. Phone in one hand: 360 px first, 44 px targets, no horizontal scroll, the important answer (open now? price?) visible without scrolling.
4. Craft over decoration: motion only to explain state (transform/opacity, interruptible, reduced-motion respected); no gradient text, no side-stripe borders, no cards inside cards.
5. Each template is a point of view: different layout, type and rhythm, not a recolour; a venue should be proud to put its name on it.
6. WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor: 4.5:1 body contrast, keyboard everything, visible focus, labelled inputs, aria-live for async states.
